# Schemami brand foundation

**Canonical strategy foundation · 10 August 2026**<br>
**Status:** founder-approved; Identity A / Bounded Savor is the production direction

> Schemami is the chosen project name. `schemami.dev` is registered and
> `github.com/dcsg/schemami` is the project repository. This document turns the
> naming decision and founder interview into a working brand system. It is not
> professional trademark clearance and it does not treat founder evidence as
> external market validation.

## Executive decision

**Schemami** is a flexible recipe protocol for software. It gives developers a
structured way to represent ingredients, methods, calculations, provenance and
constraints without prescribing the application, interface or cooking workflow
built above it.

The name combines **schema** and **umami**. That combination is the brand idea:
machine-readable structure with culinary meaning.

## Founder interview synthesis

### Origin

The project began with a practical frustration: choosing a recipe from a book
or website, then needing to translate it, convert its units and navigate it
while cooking. Work on Pão de Portugal showed the larger opportunity. If the
recipe and its method existed in a dependable representation, different teams
could build interfaces for it without rebuilding culinary meaning each time.

### Jobs the protocol must enable

- Capture recipes from books, links, photographs, PDFs, descriptions and voice.
- Preserve the method, not merely an ingredient list and instruction prose.
- Convert measures such as ounces to metric when the conversion is valid.
- Let applications localize labels and explanations from stable identities.
- Adapt a cooking plan to the cook's schedule.
- Support small substitutions and larger authored adaptations such as keto or
  sugar-free variants without pretending every change is equivalent.
- Preserve source lineage while allowing a changed recipe to become personal.
- Let applications present a simple linear recipe or a richer process without
  forcing every recipe into one archetype.

### Boundaries

- Schemami is the enabling layer, not the consumer recipe application.
- Moving recipes between applications may happen, but it is not the product's
  central purpose.
- No one should need to fill in a large protocol form. Ingestion tools and
  applications translate human inputs into candidate documents for review.
- LLM ingestion can be a paid or optional service; it is not normative behavior.
- The protocol identifies concepts. Applications localize them.
- Flexibility does not mean free-form machine semantics: unresolved values must
  remain provisional and must not masquerade as computable data.

### Success

Schemami has failed if no application integrates it. The first credible proof
is deliberately modest: Pão de Portugal plus the iPhone micro-bakery app, then
one additional integration or SDK consumer. Adoption is integration behavior,
not repository attention.

## Audience and persona hierarchy

The protocol and the products enabled by it have different audiences. The
brand must speak to the people choosing and implementing Schemami while still
showing the human consequence of their work.

### Primary brand audience — integrator

**Surface engineer / application developer**

- Builds a recipe viewer, converter, archive, cooking guide, bakery scheduler,
  publisher or domain-specific tool.
- Needs a dependable target model for ingestion and an SDK for consumption.
- Fears ambiguous fields, schema churn, silent information loss and calculations
  that behave differently across implementations.
- Wants examples, generated types, validators, conformance vectors and explicit
  loss or refusal reports.

### Secondary brand audience — maintainer

**Registry steward / protocol implementer**

- Curates stable identities and their definitions.
- Needs namespaced, versioned and federated registries rather than one central
  list pretending to cover global cooking.
- Values compatibility rules, provenance and inspectable governance.

### Indirect beneficiary — cook

**Collector and cook using an enabled application**

- Finds a recipe, imports it and follows it while cooking.
- Benefits from conversion, guided execution, schedule adaptation and personal
  variants without knowingly interacting with the protocol.
- Judges the application experience, not the elegance of the schema.

### Trust actor

**Editorial verifier**

- Protects the private-to-published boundary.
- Reviews provenance, source fidelity, ambiguity and rights.
- Needs uncertainty to remain visible and useful.

## Positioning

### Competitive frame

The practical alternatives are bespoke application schemas, unstructured recipe
documents, Schema.org Recipe, authoring languages such as Cooklang, composable
standards such as Soustack, and opinionated AI-to-execution formats such as
Cookpit.

### Positioning statement

> For developers building cooking software, Schemami is the flexible recipe
> protocol that turns ingredients and methods into dependable, computable
> structure—without prescribing the interface or forcing every recipe into the
> same workflow.

### Differentiated value

1. **Expressive:** represent linear recipes, components, fermentation, pastry,
   drinks and richer method graphs through one core with category semantics.
2. **Computable:** define scaling, bases, conversions, scheduling and guarded
   transformations as deterministic behavior.
3. **Honest:** preserve provenance, uncertainty and lineage; refuse unsupported
   calculations instead of guessing.
4. **Enabling:** let applications own ingestion, localization, interaction and
   personal recipe experiences.

### Brand promise

> **Structure the recipe without flattening the method.**

### Supporting proof line

> Ingredients, methods, calculation, provenance and constraints—one flexible
> protocol for cooking software.

### Claims discipline

Prefer evidence-sized claims:

- “Designed to represent many recipe archetypes,” not “represents every recipe.”
- “Deterministic calculation with explicit refusal,” not “perfectly safe.”
- “A recipe protocol,” not “the universal recipe standard.”
- “Publicly developed” until license and governance support “open standard.”

## Brand character

Schemami should feel:

- **Precise, not clinical.** Rules are exact because kitchen consequences matter.
- **Playful, not cute.** The name carries the wit; the interface does not need
  mascots, puns in every heading or novelty copy.
- **Curious, not culturally flattening.** Regional terms and methods remain
  specific; the protocol does not treat one culinary tradition as the default.
- **Helpful, not permissive.** A refusal explains what is missing and how to
  resolve it.
- **Calm, not bureaucratic.** Conformance and governance should feel like useful
  engineering, not certification theatre.

### Voice principles

1. Lead with the kitchen consequence; follow with the protocol mechanism.
2. Use concrete verbs: represent, resolve, scale, derive, preserve, refuse.
3. Separate fact, authored assertion, derivation and uncertainty.
4. Prefer short declarative sentences around rules and errors.
5. Teach with a real recipe fragment before presenting an abstract schema.

### Voice examples

| Context | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | “Recipes, structured to taste.” | “The AI-powered future of food interoperability.” |
| Registry | “IDs carry meaning. Interfaces provide labels.” | “Translate every recipe automatically.” |
| Refusal | “Scaling stopped: the result exceeds the recipe's authored salt bound.” | “Something went wrong.” |
| Unknown | “Technique unresolved. Preserve the source text; do not compute with it yet.” | “Other technique.” |
| SDK | “Parse, validate and calculate with the same conformance vectors.” | “Seamless, enterprise-ready integration.” |

## Naming system

### Name rationale

**Schemami = schema + umami.**

- `schema` signals structured information and developer infrastructure.
- `umami` makes the name culinary, warm and memorable without naming one app.
- The fusion is distinctive enough to carry a visual identity while the
  descriptor “recipe protocol” supplies immediate category clarity.
- The name can stretch across the protocol, SDKs, registries and documentation.

### Usage

- Use **Schemami** in prose and product names.
- Use **schemami** for packages, commands and namespaces.
- On first encounter, pair the name with **Flexible recipe protocol** or
  **Recipe protocol for software**.
- Do not revive `RCP` as the primary public signature. It is overloaded and
  loses the personality the new name creates.

### Verbal playground

The name gives the brand permission to enjoy language. The system should use
one strong line at a time, then let the technical copy be plain and exact.

**Selected verbal system**

> **Recipes, structured to taste.**

It uses “to taste” as both a cooking instruction and a promise of flexibility:
Schemami provides computable structure without forcing every recipe into one
shape. Use it as the primary tagline while testing the visual identity.

> **Give your recipes some Schemami.**

This is the active invitation: warmer, more playful and especially useful for
SDK onboarding, examples and ingestion tools.

> **Schema meets umami.**

This is the name-reveal line rather than the permanent tagline. Use it where the
brand introduces its wordplay: an About page, launch sequence, wordmark motion,
conference slide or the first section after a homepage hero.

**Supporting hooks**

| Line | Best use |
|---|---|
| **Mise en schema.** | Developer campaign, stickers, conference material |
| **Schema, served.** | Short social or release signature |
| **The recipe protocol with good taste.** | Playful introduction where the category must be explicit |
| **Add structure. Keep the flavor.** | Positioning against rigid or lossy formats |
| **Recipes, the Schemami way.** | Community/editorial language after the brand is familiar |

These are a language palette, not seven simultaneous taglines. Avoid making
every interface label a food pun; the wit should make Schemami memorable, while
the protocol language makes it trustworthy.

### Architecture

| Layer | Recommended expression |
|---|---|
| Project | Schemami |
| Normative specification | Schemami Protocol |
| Command-line tool | `schemami` |
| Language SDK | Schemami Swift · Schemami TypeScript · Schemami Go |
| Registry ecosystem | Schemami Registries |
| Conformance | Schemami Conformance |
| Secondary ingestion product | Separate product name or “Schemami Convert”; not part of normative conformance |

**Taxomami** remains a playful possible name for registry tooling—taxonomy +
umami—but should not be introduced until the primary brand is established.

## Visual strategy

> **Applied identity history:** compare **Flow S**, **Bounded Method** and
> **Recipe Fingerprint** in the
> [Schemami identity concept board](schemami-identity-concepts.html). The board
> tests each direction as a wordmark, homepage, documentation header and avatar.
> The founder selected **option 2’s bracket logo and connection nodes, option
> 1’s serif lettering, and option 3’s colors**. A later controlled decision lab
> confirmed this synthesis, **Bounded Savor**, as the production direction.

### Recognition principles

- Wordmark first. The coined name is the primary memory asset.
- Build a symbol from **inputs → transformation → output**, not a chef hat,
  fork, spoon or generic code brackets.
- Use lines, nodes, bounds and annotations to show that a recipe is both a
  document and a process.
- Let one warm accent interrupt an otherwise calm technical surface.
- Avoid indigo gradients, glass cards, food-delivery orange and generic AI
  sparkle imagery.

## Typography standard

Use **Fraunces + IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Mono** as the canonical type system.

- Fraunces is expressive and soft enough to echo the name; use it for major
  headlines, quotes and the wordmark exploration—not dense documentation.
- Plex Sans is built for interface environments and has broad script support.
- Plex Mono keeps code and identifiers in the same family as the interface.
- Both families use the SIL Open Font License. Bundle reviewed WOFF2 subsets
  rather than loading them from a third-party font CDN.
- The production package includes both Latin and Latin-Extended subsets. Test
  additional scripts before adding them; do not silently fall back in a
  supported locale.

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next remains the accessibility-led alternative if early
tests show Plex is uncomfortable in long recipe or review surfaces.

## Validation and remaining decisions

### Founder concept test — completed

The founder reviewed controlled specimens that separated color, typography and
the full challenger. Identity A was selected. The deciding evidence was:

- the original Marine palette was more comfortable for repeated reading than
  the green Ferment Lab challenger;
- the bracket-and-node mark communicated the programmatic category most
  directly;
- Fraunces plus Plex retained more ownable culinary warmth than the one-family
  Recursive system.

The original evaluation questions were:

1. What kind of project do you expect behind this identity?
2. Does it feel like developer infrastructure, a consumer recipe app, or a
   publication?
3. What do you remember after ten minutes?
4. Which direction makes the Schemami name feel inevitable rather than added?

### External validation

- Test the name and one-line description with 5–8 developers who have recently
  built recipe ingestion, storage or cooking interfaces.
- Ask for category comprehension, pronunciation and unaided spelling.
- Test a real schema/example page rather than a logo in isolation.
- Validate the promise against a recent integration defect or workaround.

### Production gates

- Professional name/trademark clearance.
- Explicit project license and governance language.
- Component-level WCAG review beyond the foundation components.
- External developer comprehension, recall and long-reading validation.
- Script and glyph expansion beyond the bundled Latin and Latin-Extended sets.
- A rename/migration plan for schema IDs, namespaces, packages and repository
  copy; this brand document does not authorize a blind search-and-replace.
